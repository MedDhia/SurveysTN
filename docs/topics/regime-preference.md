# Regime preference

What kind of political system people say they want, and what they say about the alternatives. Preference is kept apart from two things it is easily confused with: how democratic people judge their own country to be, and how democratic they rate other countries. Both are assessments, both have their own facet, and neither is a preference.

Generated by `scripts/build_topic_index.py` from the lexicon in
[`catalog/topics.json`](../../catalog/topics.json). The full table is
[`regime-preference.csv`](regime-preference.csv), one row per variable.

## What the lexicon leaves out, and why

Matching is lexical, so this finds questions that say what they are about. A
question that measures inequality without naming it — asking your income and your
father's, say — is not here, and its absence means nothing.

These words were tried and rejected as false friends:

| Word | Why not |
|---|---|
| `army` | trust in the army, armed forces as an institution, and soldiers or roadblocks recorded in the sampling unit — only army rule is a regime preference |
| `regime` | 'the fall of the Asad regime', satisfaction with the Mubarak regime — a named government, not a preference over regime types |
| `rule of law` | the state's application of the rule of law between citizens, which is governance quality |
| `political system (trust)` | trust in political institutions, which is support for incumbents rather than for a system |
| `level of democracy (elsewhere)` | Afrobarometer Round 5 and the Arab Opinion Index 2011 rate how democratic China, the USA, France, Iran, Turkey, Israel and Saudi Arabia are; those are ratings of other countries and sit in their own facet rather than in this country's assessment |

The lexicon is [`catalog/topics.json`](../../catalog/topics.json). Widen or narrow
it and re-run `scripts/build_topic_index.py`; nothing else has to change.
